# Documents to enter a country

What a country asks for depends on __OWNER__'s nationality and changes over time: a visa, an online travel
authorisation, a minimum passport validity. Check it on the destination's **official government site** for every
trip, not from memory. Unofficial sites that look official and charge extra for the same form are common: never
apply or pay through one, and say so if __OWNER__ found one.

## Where the details come from

Passport and ID numbers, expiry dates and dates of birth are only used from where __OWNER__ chose to keep them (the
line "Passport and ID details are kept in" in SKILL.md). If there is no such place, ask them each time and do not
write the details anywhere. Type them only into the official site; never into a message, a calendar event, a note
or memory.

## Check it yourself when the site allows it

If the official site lets anyone check a permit with the passport details, do it yourself and send __OWNER__ a
screenshot of the result, instead of sending them to look it up. Note here how each check works once you have used
it (made-up examples, no real details).

### Example: the UK ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation)

- Who needs it: visitors to the UK who do not need a visa (most non-British, non-Irish travellers; the gov.uk page
  says who).
- Check it at `https://www.gov.uk/check-eta`: "Start now", then nationality, passport number and expiry date, and
  date of birth. It does not ask for an application number. The answer reads like "You have an ETA. Your ETA expires
  on …". Take a screenshot of it.
- It is tied to the passport it was issued for: a new passport needs a new ETA. Apply only on gov.uk or the
  official UK ETA app.

## After the check

- Valid for the whole trip: one line to __OWNER__ with the expiry date, and the screenshot.
- Missing or expiring before the return: tell them at once with the official link to apply and how long it usually
  takes (from the official page), and put a reminder in `notes/todo.md`.
- Passport validity: many countries ask for some months left after the return date; check the rule and compare it
  with the expiry date.
